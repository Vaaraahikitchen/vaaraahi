Cooking tradition

Why Dum Biryani Takes Its Time

Dum is a way of cooking that rewards patience. Rice, spices and the rest of the ingredients finish together in a covered pot, where the heat and fragrant steam stay close instead of escaping.

That sealed pot is central to the experience: the aroma builds as everything cooks slowly, and lifting the lid becomes part of the anticipation. It is not a shortcut-friendly dish; each layer and each minute contributes to the final plate.

At Vaaraahi, that care reflects a simple idea: a meal worth sharing deserves the time it takes to make it well.

Flavor notes

A Taste of Andhra: Gongura and More

Andhra cooking is known for flavors that are lively, layered and full of personality. Tang, heat and aromatic spices each have a place, creating dishes that are memorable without tasting all the same.

Gongura, a leafy sorrel, is prized for its distinctive tartness. Alongside ingredients such as tamarind, chilies and curry leaves, it helps give many regional dishes their unmistakable character.

There is no single measure of “spicy” that suits everyone, so the best way to explore is at your own pace. Look for a dish that sounds inviting, ask about its flavor, and enjoy discovering something new.

At the table

Making Room for Everyone at the Feast

Some of the best meals are served in the middle of the table. A few dishes to pass around, a mix of familiar favorites and something new, and everyone can make a plate their own.

For a gathering, family-style portions make sharing easy. For a smaller meal, pairing a favorite curry with rice or bread is just as satisfying. A table feels welcoming when there are choices for different tastes, including vegetarian options.

However you gather, take your time, pass the plates, and save space for one more person. That is the spirit of hospitality we try to bring to every meal.
